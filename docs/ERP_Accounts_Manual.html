0.2 A–Z Accounting Terminology with Shortcuts & Examples

Every term you will encounter in the ERP — explained simply with a real-life pharma example.

A Account
Definition
A record that tracks increases and decreases of a specific item — money, asset, expense, or income.
Shortcut / Tip
Think of an account like a bucket. Money pours in and out. The balance is what's left.
Real Example
"Cash A/c" tracks all cash inflows and outflows. If you received ₹10,000 and paid ₹4,000, balance = ₹6,000.
A Asset
Definition
Anything the business owns that has monetary value. Split into Fixed Assets (long-term) and Current Assets (short-term).
Shortcut / Tip
"What we OWN = Asset"
Fixed = stays >1 year. Current = converts to cash within 1 year.
Real Example
Tablet pressing machine = Fixed Asset. Paracetamol stock in warehouse = Current Asset. Cash in hand = Current Asset.
B Balance Sheet
Definition
A financial snapshot of the business on a specific date — showing Assets on one side and Liabilities + Capital on the other.
Shortcut / Tip
Assets = Liabilities + Capital (always!). If this doesn't balance, there's an accounting error.
Real Example
On 31-Mar-2026: Assets = ₹56,45,000. Liabilities = ₹6,20,000. Capital + Profit = ₹50,25,000. Both sides = ₹56,45,000 ✅
B BRS — Bank Reconciliation Statement
Definition
A statement that explains the difference between the bank balance in your books (ERP) and the actual bank statement balance.
Shortcut / Tip
Differences happen because cheques take days to clear. BRS = "Why don't the two numbers match?"
Real Example
Your ERP shows Bank: ₹4,12,200 but bank statement shows ₹4,07,800. Difference ₹4,400 = a cheque issued to ABC Pharma not yet cleared by bank.
C Capital
Definition
Money or assets invested by the owner(s) into the business. Also called Owner's Equity or Net Worth.
Shortcut / Tip
"Capital = what the business owes back to its owner." It increases with profit and decreases with losses or withdrawals.
Real Example
Promoter invested ₹50,00,000 to start Metapharsic. This is the Capital Account. Yearly profits get added to it.
C Contra Entry
Definition
A transaction that involves both sides being cash/bank. Money moves between Cash and Bank — no external party involved.
Shortcut / Tip
Think "internal transfer." Cash to Bank or Bank to Cash. Never use for payments to outsiders.
Real Example
Accountant deposits ₹50,000 cash into SBI bank. Dr SBI Bank A/c ₹50,000 | Cr Cash A/c ₹50,000. No supplier or customer involved.
C Credit Note
Definition
A document issued when a customer returns goods or is given a discount/refund AFTER the sale invoice was issued.
Shortcut / Tip
"Credit Note = Sales Undo." It reduces the customer's outstanding and reduces your sales. Always link it to the original invoice.
Real Example
City Hospital returns 50 strips of expired Crocin (sold last week). Issue Credit Note for ₹2,500. Dr Sales Return ₹2,500 | Cr City Hospital ₹2,500.
D Debit (Dr.)
Definition
The left side of any account entry. It increases assets and expenses. It decreases liabilities, capital, and income.
Shortcut / Tip
"Dr = Left side."
Assets/Expenses go UP when debited.
Liabilities/Income go DOWN when debited.
Real Example
Paying rent ₹15,000: Debit Rent A/c (expense increases ✅). That's correct — rent expense just went up by ₹15,000.
D Debit Note
Definition
A document you send to a supplier when you return goods purchased from them or when they overcharged you.
Shortcut / Tip
"Debit Note = Purchase Undo." You debit the supplier's account (reducing what you owe them).
Real Example
You return 10 damaged vials to ABC Pharma worth ₹3,000. Dr ABC Pharma A/c ₹3,000 | Cr Purchase Return A/c ₹3,000.
D Depreciation
Definition
The gradual reduction in the value of a fixed asset over time due to wear and tear, obsolescence, or usage.
Shortcut / Tip
"Asset value falls every year = Depreciation." Post it annually as a Journal Entry. No cash is involved.
Real Example
Tablet press machine worth ₹10,00,000 depreciates 10% per year = ₹1,00,000. Dr Depreciation A/c ₹1,00,000 | Cr Machinery A/c ₹1,00,000.
E Expense
Definition
Money spent or cost incurred to run the business. Reduces profit. Classified as Direct (related to production) or Indirect (overhead).
Shortcut / Tip
Always DEBIT an expense account. Direct = part of product cost. Indirect = general overhead (rent, salary, printing).
Real Example
Printing company certificates = ₹3,500. This is an Indirect Expense → Debit Printing & Stationery A/c ₹3,500.
G GST — Goods and Services Tax
Definition
An indirect tax levied on supply of goods and services in India. Split into CGST + SGST (intra-state) or IGST (inter-state).
Shortcut / Tip
GST you collect = LIABILITY (you owe govt).
GST you pay = ASSET (Input Tax Credit).
Net = Pay to govt.
Real Example
Sell drugs worth ₹1,00,000 @ 12% GST. Collect ₹12,000 GST. But you paid ₹4,500 GST on purchase. Net GST payable = ₹12,000 − ₹4,500 = ₹7,500.
I Input Tax Credit (ITC)
Definition
The GST you paid on purchases that can be used to reduce the GST you owe on sales. Essentially a tax refund mechanism.
Shortcut / Tip
ITC = GST "already paid" voucher. You don't pay the same tax twice — deduct ITC from output GST before paying the balance.
Real Example
Paid ₹9,000 GST on drug purchase = Input CGST ₹4,500 + Input SGST ₹4,500. This ITC offsets your output GST liability before payment.
J Journal Entry
Definition
The very first step of recording a financial transaction — written in the format: what to Debit and what to Credit, with narration.
Shortcut / Tip
Format: Dr [Account] | Cr [Account] | By [reason].
Always write narration — future-you will thank present-you.
Real Example
Paid electricity bill ₹4,200 by cash.
Dr Electricity A/c ₹4,200
Cr Cash A/c ₹4,200
Narration: "Electricity bill June 2026"
L Ledger
Definition
A book (or ERP record) that contains all transactions for a specific account, showing the complete history and running balance.
Shortcut / Tip
Journal = First record. Ledger = Sorted by account. Think of journal as a diary, ledger as a filing cabinet with tabs per account.
Real Example
"Printing & Stationery Ledger" shows all printing bills paid through the year — letterheads, visiting cards, certificates — with dates and amounts.
L Liability
Definition
What the business owes to others — suppliers, banks, tax authorities. Opposite of assets.
Shortcut / Tip
"What we OWE = Liability." Current Liabilities = due within 1 year. Long-term = due after 1 year (bank loans).
Real Example
You purchased drugs worth ₹84,000 from ABC Pharma on credit. ABC Pharma A/c = ₹84,000 Cr = Current Liability (you still owe them).
N Narration
Definition
A short description written along with every voucher explaining why the transaction was made.
Shortcut / Tip
Always include: What + Who + When. Good narration = no confusion during audits. Never leave it blank.
Real Example
Bad narration: "Payment made" ❌
Good narration: "Certificate printing charges paid to Print Palace — June 2026 batch — Bill No. PP-241" ✅
O Opening Balance
Definition
The balance of an account at the start of a financial period — typically April 1st. It equals the closing balance of the previous year.
Shortcut / Tip
Opening = Last year's Closing. Enter it once at the start of FY. Never change it mid-year.
Real Example
Cash A/c closed at ₹75,000 on 31-Mar-2026. On 1-Apr-2026, Cash A/c Opening Balance = ₹75,000 Dr.
P P&L — Profit & Loss Account
Definition
A financial statement showing all income and expenses over a period to calculate whether the business made a Profit or a Loss.
Shortcut / Tip
P&L = Income − Expenses. Positive = Profit ✅. Negative = Loss ❌. Covers a period (e.g. full financial year).
Real Example
Sales ₹50,00,000 − COGS ₹30,00,000 − Indirect Expenses ₹8,00,000 = Net Profit ₹12,00,000 for FY 2025-26.
P Party
Definition
Any external person or business the company transacts with — customers (debtors), suppliers (creditors), employees, banks.
Shortcut / Tip
Every Party has a Ledger. Customer = Debtor = Dr balance. Supplier = Creditor = Cr balance. Always select the correct party in vouchers.
Real Example
"City Hospital" is a party (customer/debtor). "ABC Pharma" is a party (supplier/creditor). Both have their own ledgers in ERP.
R Receipt Voucher
Definition
A voucher used when money comes into the business — from customers, owners, loans, or any income source.
Shortcut / Tip
Money IN = Receipt Voucher. Debit Cash/Bank (asset increases). Credit the source (customer account reduces).
Real Example
City Hospital pays ₹1,20,000 via NEFT. Dr SBI Bank A/c ₹1,20,000 | Cr City Hospital A/c ₹1,20,000. Bank balance goes up ✅.
S Sundry Debtors & Creditors
Definition
Debtors = customers who owe you money (sold on credit). Creditors = suppliers you owe money to (bought on credit).
Shortcut / Tip
Debtor = "they owe US" = Asset (Dr balance).
Creditor = "we owe THEM" = Liability (Cr balance).
Real Example
Sold drugs to MediCare Clinic on credit ₹45,000 → MediCare = Debtor. Bought raw materials from ChemCo on credit ₹20,000 → ChemCo = Creditor.
T TDS — Tax Deducted at Source
Definition
A tax deducted by the payer at the time of making payment above a threshold. Deducted amount is deposited to the government on behalf of the payee.
Shortcut / Tip
TDS = "Pay less, deduct the rest for govt." Common rates: Contractor 1-2%, Rent 10%, Professional 10%. File TDS return quarterly.
Real Example
Paying CA consultant ₹50,000. TDS @10% = ₹5,000. Actual payment = ₹45,000.
Dr Consultant A/c ₹50,000 | Cr Bank ₹45,000 | Cr TDS Payable ₹5,000.
T Trial Balance
Definition
A list of all ledger closing balances arranged in two columns (Dr and Cr) to verify that the books are arithmetically correct.
Shortcut / Tip
If Total Dr = Total Cr → books are correct ✅.
If they don't match → there's an error ❌. Run it every month-end.
Real Example
Total of all Dr balances = ₹58,51,500. Total of all Cr balances = ₹58,51,500. Both match → no arithmetic errors in books ✅.
V Voucher
Definition
A document that records a financial transaction in the ERP. Types: Payment, Receipt, Journal, Contra, Purchase, Sales, Debit Note, Credit Note.
Shortcut / Tip
Voucher = digital proof of a transaction. Each voucher gets a unique auto-generated number (e.g. PV-2026-042). Never delete — always cancel.
Real Example
Paying rent by cheque → create Payment Voucher PV-2026-043. ERP stores it permanently. Auditor can pull it up anytime.

0.3 The Five Account Types — At a Glance

🏢
Personal Account
Rule: Debit the Receiver | Credit the Giver
Relates to persons — individuals, companies, banks.
Examples: City Hospital A/c, ABC Pharma A/c, SBI Bank A/c, Owner's Capital A/c.
🎯 Example: City Hospital pays us ₹1,20,000.
They are the Giver → Credit City Hospital A/c.
Bank receives the money → Debit SBI Bank A/c.
🏭
Real Account
Rule: Debit what comes In | Credit what goes Out
Relates to tangible assets — machinery, stock, cash, furniture.
Examples: Cash A/c, Drug Stock A/c, Machinery A/c, Furniture A/c.
🎯 Example: Purchased tablet press machine ₹10 lakh cash.
Machine comes IN → Debit Machinery A/c.
Cash goes OUT → Credit Cash A/c.
📋
Nominal Account
Rule: Debit all Expenses & Losses | Credit all Incomes & Gains
Relates to income, expenses, gains, losses — temporary accounts closed at year-end.
Examples: Salary A/c, Rent A/c, Sales A/c, Interest Income A/c.
🎯 Example: Paid rent ₹15,000 by bank.
Rent = Expense → Debit Rent A/c.
Bank goes OUT → Credit Bank A/c.
💰
Capital Account
Part of Personal Account | Owner = the "Person"
The owner's investment in the business. Increases with profit/additional investment. Decreases with losses/withdrawals.
Examples: Promoter Capital A/c, Share Premium A/c.
🎯 Example: Promoter invests ₹50 lakh to start Metapharsic.
Cash comes IN → Debit Cash A/c ₹50L.
Promoter is Giver → Credit Capital A/c ₹50L.

0.4 When Does an Account Increase or Decrease? — Master Table

This is the most important table in accounting. Memorise it.

Account Type Normal Balance DEBIT Effect CREDIT Effect ERP Examples
Asset Dr (Debit) ⬆️ Increases ⬇️ Decreases Cash, Bank, Stock, Machinery, Debtors
Liability Cr (Credit) ⬇️ Decreases ⬆️ Increases Creditors, GST Payable, Bank Loan, TDS Payable
Capital Cr (Credit) ⬇️ Decreases ⬆️ Increases Owner's Capital A/c, Retained Earnings
Income / Revenue Cr (Credit) ⬇️ Decreases ⬆️ Increases Sales A/c, Interest Received, Commission Income
Expense / Loss Dr (Debit) ⬆️ Increases ⬇️ Decreases Rent, Salary, Printing, Drug Purchase, Depreciation
✅ The Fastest Memory Trick: "DEALER"

Dividends (Dr normal)  |  Expenses (Dr normal)  |  Assets (Dr normal)  |  Liabilities (Cr normal)  |  Equity/Capital (Cr normal)  |  Revenue/Income (Cr normal)

→ D, E, A accounts have normal DEBIT balances.  → L, E, R accounts have normal CREDIT balances.

0.6 The Fundamental Accounting Equation

Assets = Liabilities + Capital
This equation must ALWAYS hold true. Every transaction affects at least two accounts but keeps this balanced.
ASSETS
₹56,45,000
What we own
=
LIABILITIES + CAPITAL
₹56,45,000
What we owe + invested
💡 Why This Always Balances

Every transaction has two effects (double-entry). Pay cash for rent: Cash (Asset) goes DOWN and Rent Expense goes UP — but Expense reduces Net Profit which reduces Capital, so Capital also goes DOWN by the same amount. The equation stays balanced automatically when you follow double-entry rules correctly.

🎯 Key Takeaway Before You Begin

Every single transaction in business can be broken into two questions:
1️⃣ What is coming in or going up? → That gets DEBITED.
2️⃣ What is going out or going down? → That gets CREDITED.

When you're unsure — think about the real movement of value, not just the money. Goods, services, obligations — all have value that moves between accounts.

CREDIT (Cr.)

Chapter 05

Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS)

Matching your ERP bank ledger with the actual bank statement — done monthly.

5.1 What Is BRS?

The Bank Reconciliation Statement reconciles the difference between the balance in your ERP bank ledger and the actual balance in the bank statement. Differences occur due to cheques issued but not yet cleared, deposits in transit, bank charges not yet entered, etc.

5.2 Step-by-Step BRS Process

  1. Download bank statementGet the PDF/Excel statement from your bank's portal for the month
  2. Open BRS in ERPAccounts → Bank Reconciliation → Select Bank A/c → Select Period
  3. Auto-match transactionsClick "Auto Match" — ERP compares amounts and dates automatically
  4. Manually match unmatchedFor each unmatched line, click the bank statement row and the ERP row to link them
  5. Post missing entriesIf bank shows a charge/interest not in ERP, post a journal entry for it
  6. Confirm reconciliationDifference should become ₹0. Click "Lock BRS" to finalise.

Chapter 12

Troubleshooting & Frequently Asked Questions

Common issues and their solutions for the accounts team.

12.1 Common Issues

❌ "Voucher not balanced — Dr ≠ Cr"

Cause: You entered an amount in debit but didn't complete the credit entry (or vice versa).
Fix: Check each row in the voucher. The sum of all Dr rows must equal the sum of all Cr rows before saving.

❌ "Ledger not found in dropdown"

Cause: The ledger hasn't been created yet.
Fix: Go to Accounts → Ledger Masters → Create New Ledger first, then retry the voucher.

⚠️ "Period is locked — cannot post entry"

Cause: The accounting period has been closed by the administrator.
Fix: Contact your ERP admin to temporarily unlock the period, or post the entry in the current open period.

⚠️ Trial Balance not balancing

Cause: A voucher was posted incorrectly, or opening balances have an error.
Fix: Run the "Difference Report" under Accounts → Reports → Difference Finder to identify the offending entry.

💡 GST amount calculating wrong

Cause: The product's GST rate is not set, or the party is configured as "Exempt".
Fix: Go to Inventory → Products → Edit Product → Set correct GST %. Also check party → GST Type.

12.2 FAQs

Q: Can I edit a posted voucher?
A: Yes, if the period is still open. Go to Day Book → click the voucher → click Edit. Once the period is locked, you must post a reversal journal to correct it.
Q: How do I void/cancel a voucher?
A: Open the voucher → click "Cancel Voucher". The system posts an automatic reversal entry so the audit trail is preserved. Never delete vouchers.
Q: What is a Suspense Account?
A: A temporary account used when you're unsure which ledger to debit/credit. Post the entry to Suspense A/c first, then transfer it to the correct ledger once confirmed. Clear all suspense entries before month-end.
Q: How do I record TDS deducted on payments?
A: Payment Voucher → Debit Vendor A/c full amount → Credit Bank A/c (net after TDS) → Credit TDS Payable A/c (TDS amount). Example: Paying contractor ₹1,00,000 with 10% TDS → Dr Contractor ₹1,00,000 | Cr Bank ₹90,000 | Cr TDS Payable ₹10,000.
Q: Which expense head for company certificates?
A: Use Printing & Stationery for physical printing costs. Use Legal & Professional Charges for regulatory/statutory certificates (FSSAI, ISO, drug licenses). Use Staff Welfare for employee appreciation certificates.
